Kage Loop plays videos from the user's own photo library. No account or login is required.

To test the core features:
1. Launch the app, tap "Select Video", allow photo library access, and choose any video in which someone speaks.
2. In the player, tap "Set A", let it play a few seconds, then tap "Set B". The section repeats.
3. In the word list, turn on "Show meanings", tap "Add word", type a word and tap Done. A meaning appears beneath it.

New in 1.3, adding a looped line:
4. In the word list, tap "Languages" and choose the language spoken in the video (for example English).
5. Let the A-B section repeat three times. A banner appears: "Shake to add this line". Shake the device or tap "Add", and the line is added to the word list.

This uses Apple's on-device speech recognition (SpeechAnalyzer and SpeechTranscriber, iOS 26). Only the looped section is processed, entirely on the device. The app makes no network requests of its own and sends nothing anywhere. The first use of a language may download Apple's speech model through the system, and iOS may ask for speech recognition permission. On devices below iOS 26 or without the on-device speech model, the banner never appears and everything else works as before.

Word meanings use Apple's on-device Translation framework (iOS 18 and later).

The photo library permission is used only to read videos the user selects. The app never adds to, modifies, or deletes anything in the library.

Note: the review device needs at least one video with speech in its photo library.
